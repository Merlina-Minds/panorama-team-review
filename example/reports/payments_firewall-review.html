How to read this report

This report contains two kinds of rule, and only the first kind is a question to you.

  • Your rules — rules written for your address space: something in the rule names one of your networks, or carries your team's tag or zone. You are the one who can say whether they are still needed.
    • Inbound — other systems may reach yours. Read these first: they are your exposure.
    • Outbound — your systems may reach elsewhere.
    • Both ends yours — source and destination are both networks of yours, so the traffic stays inside your own address space.
    • No direction — attributed to your team by tag, name or device group, which does not say which side you are on.
  • Rules that also cover your networks — estate-wide rules that happen to include you: ping, DNS, Active Directory and the like. Your networks sit inside a much larger one the rule names. Nothing is being asked of you here. They are listed so you can see the access you already have — there is no need to request it — and so you can speak up if one of your systems must not have it.

Addresses are shown by name, because the name is what a change request has to cite. Hold the mouse over one to see the networks and addresses behind it.

Rules are listed in the order the firewall evaluates them, grouped into the blocks it evaluates as a unit. The first matching rule wins, so a rule above another can make it irrelevant. Each block says which firewalls it applies to and how many rules it holds in total, so you can see that rules you are not shown sit between the ones you are.

Expand any rule with details for the exact object names, the resolved networks, the ports, and why the rule is in your report. Every section heading collapses. Questions about a rule, or need one changed? Raise a ticket with the firewall team and quote the rule name and its location.

Usage data not included

Rule hit counters are runtime state and are not part of a configuration backup, so this report cannot say how often a rule matched. Statements about unused rules are therefore absent rather than guessed. Hit counts can be collected separately by enabling the optional enrichment module.
